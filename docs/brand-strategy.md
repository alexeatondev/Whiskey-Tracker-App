# Brand Strategy

Status: **Approved by Dev (Nov 19, 2026).** Name: **Second Pour**, pending trademark clearance (see [decisions.md › D-002](decisions.md)).

## 1. Mission, vision and promise

**Mission**

> Help every whiskey drinker remember what they loved, understand why, and find the next bottle worth pouring.

**Vision**

> A world where nobody wastes a pour, a bottle or a great night they can't remember. Everyone carries a personal whiskey memory that knows their palate better than any critic.

**Brand promise** (what we commit to in every release)

> Snap the bottle. Remember the pour. Know what you'll love next.

**Purpose behind it:** whiskey is expensive, personal and social, but most of what people learn about their own taste disappears by the next morning. Critics and crowd scores tell you what *other people* like. We exist to make your own experience the most useful guide you have.

## 2. Positioning

> **For** whiskey drinkers, from the curious to the serious collector,
> **who** want to remember what's worth pouring again,
> **Second Pour is** the personal whiskey journal
> **that** reads any label from a photo and learns your palate.
> **Unlike** review databases and inventory apps, it's built around *your* tastings, not the crowd's, and it never caps your collection, shows ads, or holds your data hostage.

**Category we claim:** *personal palate journal*. Not "whiskey database" (Distiller, Whiskybase own that), and not "collection tracker" (crowded).

**Reasons to believe**

1. A photo of the label is all it takes: AI reads name, proof, barrel and batch, including store picks barcode scanners miss.
2. One whiskey, many tastings, so your opinion is a history and not a single score.
3. Flavor tags and ratings build a palate profile that predicts what you'll like.
4. Free core forever, no ads, no bottle caps, full export.
5. Works on iPhone, Android, Windows and Mac from one account.

## 3. Brand pillars

| Pillar | What it means | How it shows up |
| --- | --- | --- |

| **Effortless memory** | Logging should take less time than pouring. | Photo-first capture, 30-second target, smart defaults, offline |
| **Welcoming expertise** | Deep enough for the collector, never gatekeeping for the newcomer. | Plain-language descriptions, tap-to-tag flavors, no jargon required, no scores to "get right" |

| **Better together, on your terms** | Whiskey is social, but sharing is opt-in. | Share cards and tasting nights, no public feed by default |

## 4. Personality and voice

**Personality:** the friend behind the bar who remembers what you liked last time. Knowledgeable, warm, a little dry-witted, never a snob.

| We are | We are not |
| --- | --- |
| Warm, conversational | Bro-y, macho, "real men drink" |
| Knowledgeable, specific | Pretentious, jargon-first |
| Confident, concise | Salesy, hype-driven |
| Playful with whiskey idioms | Punny on every screen |
| Inclusive: whiskey is for everyone who enjoys it | Coded for one gender or age |
| Responsible | Preachy |

**Voice in practice**

| Moment | ✅ Say | ❌ Not |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Label read succeeded | "Got it: Eagle Rare 10. Have a look, then rate it." | "AI analysis complete!!! 🎉" |
| Low confidence | "I'm not sure which batch this is. Can you check the back label?" | "Error: identification failed." |
| Duplicate found | "You rated this 4.0 in March. Log another pour?" | "Duplicate entry detected." |
| Empty shelf | "Your shelf is empty. Snap your first bottle." | "No data." |
| Paywall | "You've used this month's 15 free label reads. Pro makes them unlimited, or keep typing them in for free." | "Upgrade now to unlock!" |
| Responsible use | "Enjoy responsibly. We're here for the memory, not the volume." | Moralizing pop-ups |

**Word choices:** *pour, bottle, shelf, palate, tasting, notes, finish.* Use "whiskey" by default (US audience) and accept "whisky" in Scotch, Japanese and Canadian data. Avoid "booze", "hooch", "drink like a man", "connoisseur" (gatekeepy) and "investment-grade."

## 7. Brand architecture and extensibility

- **Second Pour** is the master brand. Features keep plain names: *Shelf*, *Tasting Log*, *Palate*, *Pour Picker*, *Will I like it?*
- **Pro tier:** "Second Pour Pro". The lifetime option is the "Founding Pour" during launch.
- The name doesn't say "whiskey", so it can extend to bourbon-adjacent spirits (rum, tequila, wine) later without a rebrand. The App Store subtitle carries the whiskey keyword for search.

## 8. Go-to-market voice and channels (early)

- Enthusiast communities first: r/bourbon, r/Scotch, r/whiskey, Bourbon Women, local bourbon societies, store-pick groups on Facebook. Show up as a builder asking for feedback, not with ads.
- Share cards are the built-in growth loop: every shared card carries the name and a link.
- Midwest launch angle: Des Moines and Iowa (the state publishes open liquor product data), plus the Ohio/Indiana/Illinois/Tennessee feeder markets for the Kentucky Bourbon Trail.
- Responsible-drinking stance is part of the brand, not just legal copy: 21+ gate, no consumption gamification (no "drink streaks"), no volume leaderboards.

## Naming study

### Method

An enterprise-style process run at desk scale:

1. **Brief:** the name must be memorable, hint at whiskey without saying "whiskey", sound warm and inclusive, pass the say-it/spell-it test, stand out in a crowded App Store, and leave room to grow beyond whiskey.
2. **Generation:** about 30 candidates across descriptive, evocative, coined and idiom-based routes.
3. **Availability screen:** App Store search and US trademark spot checks. Candidates eliminated or flagged:
   - **Taken or crowded:** Pourfolio, Palate, NEAT, Dram Note / DramNote, CaskNotes, BarrelBook and Neat Notes all exist as whiskey apps. The *dram-*, *cask-*, *barrel-* and *-notes* families are saturated.
   - **Trademark risk:** "Rickhouse" is a registered mark and is used by Gold Bar Whiskey and Louisville Rickhouse Whiskey Co. "Tasteprint" is registered by Firmenich and another company.
   - **Too generic to own:** Whiskey Tracker, Encore, Angel's Share (widely used by bars and brands).
4. **Synthetic consumer test:** 12 finalists, including the current name as a control, were run through a simulated panel of 1,000 respondents weighted to our five personas (35% collector, 30% explorer, 15% palate builder, 12% traveler, 8% gift buyer). Each name was scored on six attributes: memorable, whiskey cue, clarity, distinctiveness, say/spell and tone. Each simulated respondent got individual weights drawn around their persona's priorities plus perception noise, then picked a favorite and a top three using a logit choice model (`research/name_sim.py`).
5. **Final score** = 60% consumer top-3 appeal + 20% extensibility + 20% availability.

> ⚠️ This is a **simulation of judgment, not real survey data**. The attribute scores are analyst ratings; the simulation shows how those ratings play out across segments with realistic variation. It narrows the field. A real panel test of the top three should confirm it before committing.

Collectors and travelers still gave the descriptive control name 15–22%, which is a reminder to keep "whiskey" in the App Store subtitle.

### Top 5 with commentary

1. **Second Pour** — *"Worth a second pour"* is how drinkers already praise a whiskey, so the name doubles as a rating and states the product's purpose: remembering what's worth having again. Easy to say and spell, warm, and doesn't lock us into whiskey. Risk: "pour" names are popular (Pourfolio, Pour Picks); keep the two-word form and the glass logo distinct. **Recommended.**
2. **Pourtrait** — a clever "portrait of your palate" pun and highly distinctive, but it fails the say-it/spell-it test (people hear "portrait"), which hurts word of mouth and search. It ranks high on business factors, not consumer appeal.
3. **Long Finish** — elegant, a real tasting term (a finish that lingers, like a good memory), and loved by palate-focused drinkers. It's less obvious to newcomers, and the phrase is common in wine.
4. **Proofmark** — "proof" plus a hallmark of quality; sounds trustworthy and premium. Slightly abstract, and "proof" means evidence to non-drinkers.
5. **Sipmark** — short, ownable and extensible, but weak emotional pull and a whiskey cue in the simulation; it would need heavy brand investment. Treat it as a backup.

### Validation plan (before committing)

1. USPTO knockout search for Second Pour, Long Finish and Proofmark in Classes 9 and 42. Consider a trademark attorney's clearance (~$500–$1,500) before launch spend.
2. Check domains and social handles (`secondpour.app`, `getsecondpour.com`, @secondpourapp).
3. PickFu or a similar panel: 50 US adults 21+ who drink whiskey monthly. Run a head-to-head of the top three with an open-ended "what does this app do?" question (~$50–$150).
4. Five-second test of the logo concept and app icon on a simulated App Store results page next to Distiller, OnlyDrams and Pour Picks.
